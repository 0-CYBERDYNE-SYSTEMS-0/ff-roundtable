# Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) Plant Guide for South Texas

## Introduction
This guide provides recommendations for selecting and growing plants suitable for Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) production in the South Texas region, considering the hot, semi-arid climate, water efficiency, and local growing conditions.

## Climate Considerations
- **Region**: South Texas (USDA Zones 8b-9b)
- **Climate**: Hot, semi-arid with long summers and mild winters
- **Rainfall**: Low to moderate, often erratic
- **Key challenges**: High evaporation rates, intense sun, periodic drought

## Recommended Plant Categories

### 1. Drought-Tolerant Native & Adapted Plants
These plants require minimal irrigation once established:

- **Purslane (Portulaca oleracea)**:
  - Nutrient profile: High in omega-3 fatty acids, vitamins A, C, E
  - Growth habit: Fast-growing succulent
  - Water needs: Very low
  - Harvest time: Morning after dew dries

- **Nopales (Opuntia spp.)**:
  - Nutrient profile: Rich in fiber, calcium, magnesium, antioxidants
  - Growth habit: Cactus pads (cladodes)
  - Water needs: Extremely low
  - Harvest time: Morning, young pads preferred

- **Texas Sage (Leucophyllum spp.)**:
  - Nutrient profile: High in minerals, aromatic compounds
  - Growth habit: Shrub
  - Water needs: Very low
  - Harvest time: Early morning

### 2. Fast-Growing Vegetables & Herbs
These benefit from efficient irrigation systems:

- **Sweet Potato Vines (Ipomoea batatas)**:
  - Nutrient profile: Beta-carotene, iron, vitamins B6, C, D
  - Growth habit: Vigorous vine
  - Water needs: Moderate (drip irrigation recommended)
  - Harvest time: Morning, young leaves and stems

- **Basil (Ocimum basilicum)**:
  - Nutrient profile: Vitamins A, K, manganese, essential oils
  - Growth habit: Herbaceous annual
  - Water needs: Moderate (consistent moisture)
  - Harvest time: Before flowering, morning

- **Cilantro/Coriander (Coriandrum sativum)**:
  - Nutrient profile: Vitamins A, C, K, antioxidants
  - Growth habit: Cool-season herb
  - Water needs: Moderate
  - Harvest time: Morning, before bolting in heat

### 3. Cover Crops & Weeds
These provide abundant biomass with minimal inputs:

- **Cowpea (Vigna unguiculata)**:
  - Nutrient profile: Nitrogen-rich, protein, minerals
  - Growth habit: Legume vine
  - Water needs: Low to moderate
  - Harvest time: Morning, vegetative stage

- **Sunflower Greens (Helianthus annuus)**:
  - Nutrient profile: Iron, B vitamins, chlorophyll
  - Growth habit: Fast-growing microgreens
  - Water needs: Moderate
  - Harvest time: 10-14 days after sowing, morning

## Water Management Recommendations
1. **Irrigation Methods**:
   - Drip irrigation or soaker hoses for efficient water delivery
   - Mulching (3-4 inches) to retain soil moisture
   - Rainwater harvesting systems

2. **Timing**:
   - Water early morning to minimize evaporation
   - Adjust schedule based on soil moisture sensors

## Harvest Guidelines
- **Best time**: Early morning after dew evaporates but before full sun
- **Plant stage**: Vegetative growth phase (before flowering)
- **Quantity**: 1-2 pounds fresh material per gallon of FPJ batch
- **Quality**: Healthy, disease-free growth; avoid stressed plants

## Seasonal Planting Schedule

```table
| Season  | Recommended Plants                   | Notes                          |
|---------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Spring | Cilantro, Basil, Sunflower Greens    | Start after last frost         |
| Summer | Purslane, Sweet Potato, Cowpea       | Provide afternoon shade if possible |
| Fall   | Basil, Cilantro, Purslane            | Extend season with row covers  |
| Winter | Nopales (established), Texas Sage    | Minimal growth; harvest sparingly |
```

## FPJ Preparation Notes for South Texas
1. **Fermentation considerations**:
   - Higher temperatures may accelerate fermentation
   - Monitor closely (3-5 days vs. typical 5-7)
   - Use breathable covers to prevent mold in high humidity

2. **Storage**:
   - Refrigerate FPJ immediately after straining
   - Use within 1-2 months for optimal potency
